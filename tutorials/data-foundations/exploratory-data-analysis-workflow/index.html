Exploratory Data Analysis Workflow

EDA is a disciplined investigation of structure, quality, distributions, relationships, and anomalies before formal conclusions. Learn a reproducible sequence.

◷ 55 minBeginner

What you will learn

  • Separate discovery from confirmation.
01

Purpose of EDA

Exploratory data analysis uses summaries and graphics to understand data structure, detect problems, generate hypotheses, assess assumptions, and identify patterns worth deeper study. It is not a fishing license for unsupported stories; findings are provisional until checked.

02

A reliable sequence

Start with question and scope. Inventory files, rows, fields, types, keys, and time coverage. Profile missingness and validity. Examine numerical distributions and categorical frequencies. Compare important groups and time patterns. Investigate anomalies. Document decisions and open questions.

03

Univariate, bivariate, multivariate

Univariate analysis studies one variable: distribution, center, spread, and unusual values. Bivariate analysis studies relationships between two variables. Multivariate analysis considers several variables together. Begin simple; complexity should answer a question, not decorate a report.

04

Exploration versus confirmation

Patterns discovered in the same data can be unstable. When decisions are high-stakes, validate findings with new data, pre-specified tests, holdout periods, or sensitivity analysis. Clearly label exploratory findings.

Vocabulary

Key terms

EDA

Systematic exploration of data using summaries and visualizations.

Univariate

Analysis of one variable at a time.

Bivariate

Analysis of the relationship between two variables.

Sensitivity analysis

Checking whether conclusions change under reasonable alternative choices.

Example

EDA of retail sales

The retail dataset contains order date, region, category, quantity, revenue, discount, and profit.

  1. 1

    Confirm row grain and date coverage.

  2. 2

    Profile missing, invalid, and duplicate records.

  3. 3

    Summarize revenue, profit, quantity, and discount distributions.

  4. 4

    Compare categories and regions using counts, totals, medians, and rates.

  5. 5

    Examine time patterns and investigate extreme losses.

  6. 6

    Write findings, limitations, and next questions.

Conclusion

EDA creates an evidence map and quality report; it does not automatically prove why patterns occurred.

What to remember

  • Quality checks come before interpretation.
  • Move from simple summaries to focused comparisons.
  • Exploratory patterns are provisional.

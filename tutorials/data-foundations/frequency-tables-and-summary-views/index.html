Frequency Tables and Summary Views

Frequency tables turn raw values into counts and proportions. Learn categorical and grouped numerical tables, cumulative frequency, and responsible summaries.

◷ 45 minBeginner
ObjectivesLearnExampleTry itExercisesSummary
Start here

What you will learn

  • Create frequency and relative-frequency tables.
  • Group numerical values into defensible intervals.
  • Interpret cumulative frequency.
  • Avoid misleading bins and denominators.
01

Counts and proportions

A frequency table lists each category or interval and its count. Relative frequency divides each count by the relevant total, making groups with different sizes easier to compare. Always state the denominator and treatment of missing values.

02

Grouped numerical data

Continuous or wide-ranging numerical values can be grouped into intervals. Bins should be mutually exclusive, collectively cover the intended range, and be meaningful for the question. Different cut points can change the visual story, so inspect raw values and justify bins.

03

Cumulative frequency

Cumulative frequency adds counts up to a category or upper boundary. It answers questions such as “How many orders were delivered within three days?” It requires ordered categories or numerical intervals.

04

Summary views are choices

A frequency table reduces detail to reveal distribution. It can also hide rare values, subgroup differences, or time patterns. Use it with raw-data checks, missingness counts, and suitable charts.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Frequency

The count of observations in a category or interval.

Relative frequency

Frequency divided by the relevant total.

Cumulative frequency

Running total of ordered frequencies.

Bin

An interval used to group numerical values.

Example

Order value distribution

Order values: 450, 620, 780, 820, 1,050, 1,200, 1,450, 1,900, 2,400, 5,500.

  1. 1

    Choose intervals related to business use: below 1,000; 1,000–1,999; 2,000–2,999; 3,000 or more.

  2. 2

    Count orders in each interval.

  3. 3

    Divide by 10 for relative frequencies.

  4. 4

    Notice that the last bin contains a high-value order but hides its exact magnitude.

  5. 5

    Inspect the raw maximum alongside the grouped table.

Conclusion

The table communicates distribution compactly, but it should not replace examination of unusual values.

Summary

What to remember

  • Frequency is a count.
  • Relative frequency uses a stated denominator.
  • Bins shape grouped numerical summaries.
  • Grouped views can hide important detail.

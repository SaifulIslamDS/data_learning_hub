Data Ethics, Privacy, and Responsible Use

Legal permission is not the only test. Learn purpose limitation, data minimization, privacy, security, fairness, transparency, and responsible communication.

◷ 55 minBeginner
ObjectivesLearnExampleTry itExercisesSummary
Start here

What you will learn

  • Identify ethical risks across the data lifecycle.
  • Apply purpose limitation and data minimization.
  • Distinguish anonymisation from simple removal of names.
  • Communicate uncertainty and limitations honestly.
01

Purpose and proportionality

Collect and use data for a clear, legitimate purpose. Ask whether each field is necessary and whether a less intrusive method can answer the question. Data collected for one purpose should not silently be repurposed in ways people would not reasonably expect.

02

Privacy and security

Privacy concerns appropriate collection, access, use, sharing, and retention. Security protects data against unauthorized access, alteration, and loss. Removing names is not always anonymisation: combinations of age, location, date, or rare events may re-identify people.

03

Fairness and representation

Data and models can disadvantage groups through missing coverage, historical inequity, biased labels, unsuitable proxies, or unequal error rates. Assess subgroup performance and harms, not only overall accuracy. Avoid using protected or sensitive characteristics without a defensible need and safeguards.

04

Responsible communication

Do not exaggerate causality, precision, or certainty. Show denominators, time periods, definitions, and important uncertainty. Avoid visual scales and selective filters that mislead. State who may be missing and what decision the evidence cannot support.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Data minimization

Collecting and retaining only data necessary for a defined purpose.

Anonymisation

Processing data so individuals are no longer identifiable with reasonably available means.

Fairness

Assessment of whether data use creates unjustified unequal benefits, errors, or harms.

Purpose limitation

Using data only for specified, legitimate, compatible purposes.

Example

Publishing a healthcare dashboard

A public dashboard would show monthly visits by area, age group, diagnosis category, and clinic.

  1. 1

    Confirm public reporting purpose and legal basis.

  2. 2

    Remove unnecessary fields and use aggregated groups.

  3. 3

    Assess small cells and combinations that may identify individuals.

  4. 4

    Apply access, suppression, and retention controls.

  5. 5

    Review subgroup fairness and stigmatizing labels.

  6. 6

    Publish definitions, coverage, and limitations.

Conclusion

Responsible publication balances usefulness with privacy, fairness, and the risk of harm.

Summary

What to remember

  • Use data for clear and compatible purposes.
  • Minimize collection and access.
  • Removing names may not prevent re-identification.
  • Fairness and honest communication are analytical responsibilities.

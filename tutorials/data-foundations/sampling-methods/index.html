Sampling Methods

Sampling design controls who has a chance to appear in the data. Compare probability and non-probability methods and learn when each can or cannot support generalization.

◷ 50 minBeginner
ObjectivesLearnExampleTry itExercisesSummary
Start here

What you will learn

  • Explain simple random, systematic, stratified, and cluster sampling.
  • Recognize convenience and voluntary-response samples.
  • Choose a sampling design for a purpose.
  • Identify coverage and nonresponse concerns.
01

Probability sampling

Probability sampling uses a known random mechanism so each eligible population unit has a known, nonzero chance of selection. Simple random sampling selects units directly; systematic sampling selects every kth unit after a random start; stratified sampling samples within important subgroups; cluster sampling selects natural groups and observes units within them.

02

Non-probability sampling

Convenience, purposive, quota, and voluntary-response samples do not provide known selection probabilities. They can be useful for exploration, rapid feedback, expert cases, or hard-to-reach groups, but generalization to a wider population requires caution and explicit limitations.

03

Stratification versus clustering

Stratification ensures representation from each important subgroup and samples within every stratum. Cluster sampling selects only some groups, often to reduce travel or cost. Confusing them can lead to incorrect analysis and standard errors.

04

Sampling frame and response

A sampling frame is the operational list or mechanism used to select units. If it excludes part of the population, coverage error occurs. Even a random sample can become biased if selected people do not respond and nonrespondents differ systematically.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Simple random sample

A probability sample in which eligible units are selected randomly.

Stratified sample

Samples are drawn separately within defined subgroups.

Cluster sample

Natural groups are sampled, then units within selected groups are studied.

Sampling frame

The list or mechanism from which the sample is selected.

Example

Surveying two education centers

A program wants student satisfaction estimates and must represent both centers and grade levels.

  1. 1

    Define the eligible student population.

  2. 2

    Create strata by center and grade.

  3. 3

    Randomly sample within each stratum.

  4. 4

    Track selected, contacted, and responding students.

  5. 5

    Weight or qualify results if sampling fractions or response differ substantially.

Conclusion

Stratification protects representation of important subgroups but does not remove measurement or nonresponse error.

Summary

What to remember

  • Probability sampling uses a known random mechanism.
  • Stratification samples within all defined subgroups.
  • Cluster sampling selects some natural groups.
  • Coverage and nonresponse still matter.

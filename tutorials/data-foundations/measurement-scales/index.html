Measurement Scales

Nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio scales describe what comparisons and arithmetic are defensible. Learn the hierarchy through practical variables.

◷ 40 minBeginner
ObjectivesLearnExampleTry itExercisesSummary
Start here

What you will learn

  • Define the four common measurement scales.
  • Match variables to defensible operations.
  • Avoid treating ordinal codes as precise distances.
  • Explain why zero matters for ratios.
01

Nominal and ordinal

Nominal values are names without inherent order: blood group, district, or product type. Ordinal values have order but unequal or unknown spacing: satisfaction levels, education stage, or priority. You can compare rank, but the distance from “satisfied” to “very satisfied” is not guaranteed equal to another step.

02

Interval and ratio

Interval scales have meaningful equal differences but no true zero, such as temperature in Celsius; 20°C is not twice as hot as 10°C. Ratio scales have equal intervals and a meaningful zero, such as weight, duration, quantity, or revenue, so ratios can be meaningful.

03

Why scale matters

Measurement scale guides valid summaries and models. Nominal data use counts; ordinal data often use medians and rank methods; interval and ratio data support differences and many numerical summaries. Real variables may require domain judgment.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Nominal

Categories without inherent order.

Ordinal

Ordered categories with unknown or unequal spacing.

Interval

Equal differences are meaningful, but zero is not an absence.

Ratio

Equal differences and a meaningful zero support ratios.

Example

Four variables, four scales

Analyze payment_method, service_priority, temperature_c, and order_value.

  1. 1

    payment_method is nominal.

  2. 2

    service_priority (low, medium, high) is ordinal.

  3. 3

    temperature_c is interval.

  4. 4

    order_value is ratio because zero means no monetary value and ratios are interpretable.

Conclusion

The scale determines which comparisons are logically defensible.

Summary

What to remember

  • Nominal means labels without order.
  • Ordinal means ordered categories.
  • Interval supports differences, not meaningful ratios.
  • Ratio has a meaningful zero.

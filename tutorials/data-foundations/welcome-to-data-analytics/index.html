Welcome to Data Analytics

Data analytics turns questions and recorded evidence into explanations, decisions, and actions. This chapter shows the complete learning journey and the habits of a responsible analyst.

01

What data analytics does

Data analytics is the disciplined process of asking a useful question, collecting or locating relevant data, checking its quality, transforming it, exploring patterns, applying appropriate methods, and communicating what the evidence supports. It is not merely making charts or calculating averages.

Example

A school may ask why attendance fell. The analyst compares dates, classes, weather, holidays, and recording practices before recommending action.

02

The analyst workflow

A reliable workflow usually follows: define the decision, translate it into analytical questions, understand the data, clean and validate, explore, analyze, interpret, communicate, and document. Real work is iterative: a chart may expose a data issue that sends you back to cleaning.

03

Learning by doing

Read each chapter in sequence, run the example, change one thing, complete the exercises, and explain the result in your own words. The goal is not memorizing definitions; it is building a repeatable way to reason with data.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Analytical question

A question that can be investigated with data and has a defined population, measure, comparison, or time period.

Which product category had the highest gross margin last quarter?

Evidence

Observed or calculated information relevant to a claim.

Insight

A contextual explanation of a meaningful pattern, not just a number.

Recommendation

A proposed action linked to evidence, constraints, risks, and expected effect.

Example

From complaint to analysis

A manager says, “Sales are bad.” That is a concern, not yet an analytical question.

  1. 1

    Clarify what “bad” means: revenue, units, margin, or target attainment.

  2. 2

    Define the period and comparison: this month versus last month or budget.

  3. 3

    Identify dimensions: region, channel, product, and salesperson.

  4. 4

    Check whether returns, discounts, or missing transactions distort the result.

  5. 5

    Report the pattern and practical consequence.

Conclusion

A better question is: “Which regions and product categories explain the revenue gap against target in July, after returns?”

Summary

What to remember

  • Analytics connects a decision to evidence.
  • A vague concern must become a measurable question.
  • The workflow is iterative, not perfectly linear.
  • Interpretation and documentation are part of analysis.

Population, Sample, Parameter, and Statistic

Learn the language of inference: the population is the full group of interest, a sample is observed, a parameter describes the population, and a statistic describes the sample.

◷ 45 minBeginner

Start here

What you will learn

  • Define population and sample.
  • Distinguish parameter and statistic.
  • Define a target population clearly.
  • Explain sampling uncertainty.
01

Population and sample

The population is the complete set of people, events, transactions, or measurements about which a question is asked. A sample is the subset actually observed for analysis. The target population must include boundaries such as location, eligibility, and time.

02

Parameter and statistic

A parameter is a numerical characteristic of a population, such as the true mean income of all eligible households. It is often unknown. A statistic is calculated from sample data, such as the sample mean, and is used to estimate the parameter.

03

Sampling uncertainty

Different valid samples from the same population generally produce different statistics. That natural variation is sampling uncertainty. It is not automatically a mistake; inference uses probability and standard errors to quantify it.

04

Census is not always perfect

A census attempts to observe every population member, but can still have missing records, measurement error, duplication, timing mismatch, and processing error. A carefully designed sample may sometimes be more feasible and reliable for a specific question.

Vocabulary

Key terms

Population

The entire defined group about which conclusions are intended.

Sample

The observed subset of the population.

Parameter

A numerical characteristic of a population.

Statistic

A numerical summary calculated from a sample.

Example

Employee satisfaction survey

An organization has 500 eligible employees and surveys 120 selected employees.

  1. 1

    The 500 eligible employees are the target population.

  2. 2

    The 120 respondents form the observed sample if all selected responded.

  3. 3

    The true average satisfaction of all 500 is a parameter.

  4. 4

    The average among the 120 is a statistic.

  5. 5

    Nonresponse or poor selection can make the statistic biased, beyond ordinary sampling variation.

Conclusion

A statistic estimates a parameter only under a defensible sampling and measurement process.

Summary

What to remember

  • The population is the full defined group.
  • The sample is the observed subset.
  • Parameters describe populations.
  • Statistics describe samples and vary across samples.
